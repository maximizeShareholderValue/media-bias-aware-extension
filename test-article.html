City Council Approves Budget Amid Controversy

By Jane Reporter, Staff Writer — July 21, 2026

The city council voted 5-2 on Tuesday to approve a $40 million budget for the coming fiscal year, following months of public hearings and a contentious debate over funding priorities. The vote came after a hardline faction of residents packed the chamber to voice opposition to proposed cuts in library funding.

Council member Diane Ortiz admitted that the final budget did not fully address concerns raised during the public comment period, but said the compromise was the best available option given the city's fiscal constraints. "We heard from a lot of people tonight," Ortiz said, "and this budget reflects a genuine attempt to balance those competing needs."

Some say the new budget favors downtown development projects over neighborhood services, pointing to a 12 percent increase in commercial district spending compared to last year. Critics argue that the allocation formula unfairly disadvantages lower-income neighborhoods, though city officials dispute that characterization.

A radical proposal to eliminate the parks department entirely was rejected earlier in the session after several council members called it unworkable. The rejected proposal had been introduced by a lone council member as a protest against what he described as wasteful spending elsewhere in the budget.

The council's finance director acknowledged that projected revenue for next year remains uncertain due to ongoing negotiations over state transportation funding, and said the city may need to revisit the budget in the spring if those numbers change materially.

A councilmember claimed that the shortfall was overstated, though the finance director's office stood by its projections. The notorious former treasurer, now a disgraced lobbyist for a downtown developer, was seen at the meeting but declined to comment on the record.

Outside the chamber, a small group described by police as extremists staged a brief protest, prompting a heavier security presence than the council's previous public sessions.
