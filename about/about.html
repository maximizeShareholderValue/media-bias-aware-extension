02 · Rules

What is a rule?

A rule is one small, readable instruction that says what to look for and when it counts. When the popup says “scanned against 29 rules”, that is how many such instructions each paragraph was checked against. They are plain JSON in data/pattern-catalog.json, so anyone can read, test or change them.

03 · Method

Three tests, all must pass.

D(s, r) = L and S and C

L · Lexical

The text contains the rule's trigger word or phrase, for example claims.

S · Structure

The grammar fits, judged by a part-of-speech tagger. For example, the trigger is used as a verb, not a noun.

C · Context

Nearby words don't rule it out: not a proper name, an idiom, or a statement already attributed to a named source.

Same word, different outcome

Flagged · AF-001

The senator claims that the bill will pass.

“claims” is a reporting verb that casts doubt.

Not flagged

He rejected the claims that the ship was damaged.

“claims” is a noun here, so it is neutral.

From page to highlight

  1. Find the article.Readability.js strips menus, ads and comments.
  2. Tag the words.Compromise.js labels nouns, verbs and sentences, locally.
  3. Test every rule.Candidates must pass L, S and C.
  4. Skip quoted speech.If a quoted person said it, the outlet didn't. Slogans are the exception.
  5. Resolve overlaps.Longest span wins, then higher confidence tier, then earliest start, then lowest rule ID. Losers go to an audit log.
  6. Highlight.Hover a highlight to see the technique, the reason, a neutral wording, and the rule ID.

No bias score

Phrases Found counts highlighted spans; Techniques counts how many of the 14 they cover. A single number would hide which wording was flagged and why.

Confidence tiers

Each rule has a tier (high, medium, low) set by the authors. It only decides which rule wins when two overlap. It is not a probability.

Quoted speech

Words inside quotation marks (straight, curly or single-quoted headlines) are not flagged: the tool looks at how the outlet writes, not at what the people it quotes say.

04 · Outlets

Where outlet data comes from.

Leaning

From Media Bias/Fact Check ratings for about 8,800 outlets. It describes the outlet overall, not this one article.

Left / Center / Right

The seven-point scale folds into three: Extreme Left, Left and Center-Left are Left; Center-Right, Right and Extreme Right are Right.

Ownership

Filled in by the research team for the most-read outlets. Elsewhere it says “Ownership not recorded”; Learn about ownership opens the outlet's MBFC review.

05 · Compare

Comparing coverage.

Optional, and off until you add your own free NewsAPI key in Settings. It only runs when you open the Compare tab.

  • The article is reduced to 2–4 topic keywords on your device. Only those are searched.
  • It searches widely read, rated outlets first, then loosens the query (up to three requests). Results are cached for 30 minutes.
  • Matches are sorted into Left, Center and Right. Unrated outlets, and the outlet you are reading, are left out.
  • If NewsAPI doesn't answer within 1.5 seconds you'll see “Comparative Data Unavailable” and the reason.
  • NewsAPI's free plan only covers articles roughly 1 to 30 days old. Newer stories haven't arrived yet; older ones are out of reach.

07 · Limits

Honest about accuracy.

  • Rules are heuristics. Some flags will be legitimate wording in context. The tooltip explains the rule so you can judge.
  • Some phrases are the cue itself (“sources say”, “everyone knows”). A keyword rule can't tell neutral from persuasive use.
  • Not every technique is covered. Techniques that need the whole article, such as Repetition, aren't detected automatically yet.
  • English only.
  • How accuracy is measured. Human validators judge each flagged phrase valid or not valid (valid when at least two of three agree). We report precision overall and per technique, the false-positive rate, and agreement between validators (Fleiss' kappa).
  • Wrong flags get fixed, not ignored. Each false flag found in testing becomes a regression test and the rule's condition is tightened.
